Continuing with my Data Visualization from Project 1, here, I will look at Taylor Swift song lyrics.

Taylor Swift gets a lot of hate for supposedly overusing “car” as a rhyme with “bar”. I am very skeptical of this criticism, and so I will analyze how frequently she actually does this.

R Code 
car_bar_data <- taylor_album_songs |>
  mutate(car = str_detect(lyrics, "\\b[Cc]ars?\\b")) |>
  mutate(bar = str_detect(lyrics, "\\b[Bb]ars?\\b")) |>
  mutate(car_bar =  car & bar) |>
  mutate(car_or_bar =  car | bar)

This graph and table suggest that in the vast majority of her songs, Taylor Swift actually does not rhyme car with bar. In fact, even in her album Lover, which has the most instances of songs where Taylor rhymes car with bar, only 11.1 percent of the songs contain a car-bar rhyme. This seems to be enough evidence to dispute the claim that Taylor Swift is constantly rhyming car with bar

This table clearly shows that very few Taylor Swift songs actually feature car-bar rhymes (only 2.11 percent!), or 7 songs in total. It also shows Taylor is less likely to use bar on it’s own. 15.9% of the there time cars are mentioned, they are mentioned as a rhyme with bar, but 36.8% of the time bars are mentioned are they mentioned as a rhyme with car.

However, I do not believe that repeatedly rhyming cars with bars is initself a problem. Critics usually complain that rhyming cars with bars is indicative of lazy songwriting. Thus, if rhyming cars with bars is truly a valid critique, we would expect songs that rhyme cars with bars to be less musically varied than Taylor’s songs that do not rhyme cars with bars. In the next plot, we will compare the spread of 5 different ratings for Taylor Swift songs that rhyme car with bar, and those that don’t.

all_ars_bad <- as.character(taylor_album_songs$lyrics) |> str_extract_all("\\b\\w+(?<!e)ars?\\b")

all_ars <- all_ars_bad |> unlist()

# I also had to look up a function that would make the list into a data frame
#source - https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/r-language/how-to-convert-a-list-to-a-dataframe-in-r/

all_ars_data <- as.data.frame(list(raw_words = all_ars)) |>
  mutate(lowercase_ars = str_to_lower(raw_words)) |>
  mutate(all_singular_ars = ifelse(str_detect(lowercase_ars, "s\\b"),str_sub(lowercase_ars, 1, str_length(lowercase_ars)-1), lowercase_ars)) |>
  group_by(all_singular_ars)|>
  summarize(ar_counts = n())


ggplot(all_ars_data, aes(x = fct_reorder(all_singular_ars, ar_counts), y = ar_counts)) +
geom_col(fill = "#2aaaa2") +
labs(
